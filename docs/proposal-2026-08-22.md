# What to build next, and why — PROPOSAL, not decided

Written 2026-08-22, after a day spent in the course-import, scorecard and
scoring-engine code. Everything here is argued from something actually observed
in the codebase or in real data, not from a feature list.

## The one-sentence version

**The app is excellent at running a tournament and forgets it the moment it
ends.** Everything below follows from closing that.

## What is already strong — do not rebuild it

Sixteen formats with real engines, matrix-swept from a field of one. WHS course
handicap off per-tee slope and rating with per-format allowances. Match play
that reconciles a 5&4 result against an incomplete card. Skins, side games and
a settle-up that calculates and never moves money. Tee sheets, printed cards,
flights, brackets, cuts, carry-forward. Messages scoped from the whole club down
to one player. Weekly sign-up. A roster that carries between events. Course
library with import, refusal and verification.

That is more tournament machinery than most clubs will ever use. **Adding a
seventeenth format is not the gap**; the escape hatch already handles whatever a
club invents.

## The gap

A club runs twenty rounds a season. At the end of it the app holds twenty cards
per member and the club still types every handicap in by hand, has no record of
who won what, and starts next season with an empty screen.

Golf Genius has the same shape of hole, and it is the reason clubs treat this
category as something they rent per event rather than somewhere their golf
lives.

## Proposed, in the order I would build them

### 1. Per-round handicap override, with previous rounds locked

Already specified in `docs/requirement-per-round-handicap.md` and not built.

It is first because it is a **prerequisite**: anything that computes from
history needs history that cannot move underneath it. Today, changing a member's
handicap silently re-scores every round they have played.

Small, decided, and unblocks everything below.

### 2. A club handicap that updates itself from returned cards

The app already holds every input WHS uses: gross score, the tee's Course Rating
and Slope, the holes played. The score differential is
`(113 / slope) x (gross - CR)` and we can compute it today.

**It must not be called a WHS Index.** That is licensed, association-held data —
`docs/requirement-course-import.md` establishes this and it does not change. It
is a *club handicap*, from cards this club holds, labelled as such, and it never
overwrites a GHIN figure where one exists.

This is the compounding one. It turns twenty cards from an archive into the
thing that runs next week's competition, and it is the strongest argument
against a club going back to a spreadsheet.

### 3. The club's record — honours board, season standings, a member's history

Mostly a query over data already stored. Who won the Club Championship, every
year the app has been running. A member's own page: rounds played, best gross,
their record against a given opponent.

Cheap to build, and it is what makes software feel like the club's memory rather
than a tool. Nothing in the competitive set does this well, and no club will
migrate away from their own honours board.

### 4. Team and pair score entry

Specified in `docs/requirement-team-score-entry.md` and not built. Alternate
shot and its relatives are entered today as individual cards, which is not what
the side actually played.

### 5. Photograph a course card

The app already reads a *player's completed card* from a photo. Pointing the
same capability at a club's blank card closes the half of course import that
pasting cannot reach.

Evidence, not a guess: Green Crest publishes its card as a PDF in an iframe, and
of 527 Ohio courses in the public directory only 288 arrived with a card this
app will score against.

### 6. Non-US courses

The current source is US-only, confirmed from its own spec. GolfCourseAPI covers
~30k worldwide on a 50-request/day free tier — useless for bulk and ample for a
club importing its own course once, as a second on-demand provider.

## What I would NOT build

- **More formats.** Sixteen plus an escape hatch is past the point of return.
- **A payments rail.** Hard rule 7, and the record is the valuable half anyway.
- **Bulk-loading the world.** Search already reaches every course the source
  has; a pre-loaded list of untrustworthy cards is the bundled-courses mistake
  at scale.

## The honest risk

Items 2 and 3 change what the app IS — from a tournament tool to the club's
system of record. That is the differentiator, and it is also a larger surface to
get wrong. Item 1 first is what makes it safe to attempt.
