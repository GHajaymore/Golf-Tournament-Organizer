## Sweep 10 — security, the front door and access (2026-10-11)

## Sweep 11 — one page, drawn twice (2026-10-11)

A client screen is drawn on the server and drawn again in the browser, and React throws the
server's work away (#418) when the two differ. That fault had struck six screens, never the same
one twice. Two ways it can happen were swept as classes, and one cause was measured:

- **A screen that reads the clock while drawing.** `no-client-screen-draws-from-its-own-clock.test.ts`
  finds every helper whose last argument defaults to now, and refuses a client file that calls one
  without it. Three did: the "asked … ago" lines on the first-tournament screen, the club's
  tournament list on the player's Events, and the round-deadline control on Rounds & formats. Each
  is handed the server's day now.
- **A date or an amount the two sides spell differently.** Node and Chromium carry different
  locale data. `e2e/formats-agree.spec.ts` bundles the app's own formatters, runs them in Node and
  in the page, and holds the two to the same bytes over every locale and currency the pickers
  offer: 688 formats. It found the one the control crawl had tripped on — a Korean date range,
  "14일~16일" on the server and "14일 ~ 16일" in the browser, so a tournament set to Korea mismatched
  on every load. `formatDayRange` writes the spaced form everywhere now.
- Every page of every spec and probe records what differed when a #418 fires

## Sweep 12 — whose day it is (2026-10-11)

Found by asking what every run had in common: the browser, the server and the club were one
machine. In production the server is on UTC and no club is.

**Every deadline was a day on the server's clock.** For a club on the east coast of the United
States, entries "closing on the 12th" closed at 8pm on the 12th; the weekly "answer by Tuesday"
shut at 8pm on Tuesday; and Saturday's round stopped being "today" at 8pm with groups still out.
For a club in Sydney the same things ran eleven hours late.

What changed:

- A club has a **time zone** (migration 96, `Organization.timeZone`). A club with none takes its
  organizer's device zone the first time they open the console, and never a second time. Club
  settings shows it with the time at the club — the one thing an organizer can check — and
  changes it.
- "What day is it" is asked of the club everywhere the server asked itself: the public entry
  form and the action behind it, a member's Enter and Withdraw, the weekly In/Out answer, the
  round a tournament is "on", the member's Today, calendar and Events, the organizer's
  Registration and Rounds screens, and the "you have a tournament round today" warning.
- The helpers that ask the question have no clock of their own any more. The compiler refuses a
  call that does not say what day it is.

## Sweep 13 — the actions nobody is checked for (2026-10-11)

Breaking the time-zone setting on purpose (deleting its role check) left every guard green, which
asked a wider question: which actions have NO staff gate at all? 32 of 264. Each was read. Thirty

Two findings:

- **Nothing held a club-level action to a ROLE.** `audit-idor` asks whether a row id belongs to

  arguments are refused by validation before a missing check could matter. All nineteen club
  actions were right. `club-actions-check-the-role.test.ts` now says so.
- **One account could spend the course directory's whole day.** The live directory allows 500
  requests a day for the whole app. Searching it was counted per person — unless the person was
  an organizer, and anybody can make a club and a tournament and is then one. Importing a course
  by id was not counted for anybody: an id the stored catalogue does not hold went straight to
  the directory. Now every door asks one budget — thirty an hour for a player, 120 for an

  `the-directory-allowance-is-everyones.audit.test.ts` counts the requests that leave.

## Sweep 14 — moving by thumb, not by address (2026-10-11)

**A member who took over somebody's phone was shown that person's screens.** Measured on the
seeded club, comparing the screen with the one the second member is shown in a clean browser:
Money, My card and the Board ("YOU T7") were the FIRST member's, three of three.

The service worker answered every in-app page request from its cache first, and the cache was
keyed by address alone. The same rule made everybody's own screens stale: opening the app and
tapping a tab showed that tab as it was the last time the app was opened.

Why nothing saw it: every spec opens a screen by its address, which is a whole document and
never came from that cache. A player taps a tab, which always did. And why I had called it safe
earlier the same night (Sweep 11 said so; it was wrong): that measurement looked for a NAME on
the screen. A money page names the other people in the pot; whose page it is, is in the figures.

What changed (`public/sw.js`): **the worker keeps nobody's pages.**

The cache existed so a tab would still open with no signal. Measured with the old worker and
everything cached, it did not do that either — a tab tapped with no signal did not open. So
nothing is given up by keeping nothing:

- A page request is not touched by the worker. The network answers it, or nothing does.
- Only four files that are nobody's are kept: the icons and the manifest.
- The old address-keyed cache is deleted the first time the new worker runs on a phone.

A first version of this fix kept each person's pages under an owner and emptied them when the
phone changed hands. It was taken out the same hour: it protected a feature that did not work,
with machinery that could go wrong. Scoring with no signal never depended on the worker — the
card on screen keeps working and its strokes wait in the phone's own storage.

## Sweep 15 — the engine on every iPhone (2026-10-11)

Every run had been Chromium. Most players hold an iPhone, which is WebKit. `E2E_ENGINE=webkit`
swaps the browser under the same three project names, and the suite was started on it for the
first time.

Found in the APP:

- **A British date range threw a page error on every iPhone.** The server wrote "14 – 16 May
  2026" and WebKit "14–16 May 2026" — the same difference that Korean had with Chromium, in the
  locale most of the clubs use. The dash between two days is closed up everywhere now.
  `formats-agree` runs on both engines: 580 formats, 0 differ.
- **Tapping through a screen raised an error on it.** Safari rejects a request whose page is left
  before it answers; Chromium drops it. Three requests the app fires without waiting had no
  catch — the one-time time-zone offer, and opening a conversation or previewing a text on
  Messages.

Found in the TEST BENCH, and named so they are not read as the app's:

- WebKit does not send a Secure cookie to the plain-http test server, so signing up, setting a
  password and a round code signed nobody in. `E2E_PLAIN_HTTP=1` drops the flag for such a run;
  it is refused on Vercel (`secure-cookies.ts`).
- WebKit reports the framework's own dropped requests as errors on the page being left.
- The landing contrast measurement needs a canvas this build lacks.

NOT covered: Firefox (the browser would not start on this machine); a real iPhone, which is
WebKit with Apple's own networking and locale data rather than this build's; and CI, which
installs Chromium only — the WebKit pass is run by hand until the workflow installs it.

## Sweep 16 — typed before the page had loaded (2026-10-11)

**A round code typed on a slow connection left Continue grey for ever.** A screen is on the
phone before its script is; on one bar of signal that gap is seconds. The box showed every
letter, and the page's own record of the box stayed empty, because the letters arrived before
anything was listening. On the sign-up form the first press of Create account then ERASED the
name and address that had been typed and asked for them.

Found because three round-code specs on WebKit typed faster than the page started. Measured with
the script held back three seconds: Chromium does exactly the same. Every spec types into a page
that loaded long ago, from a server next door.

`TypedBeforeLoad` (root layout) fixes it for every text box in the app at once: a tiny script
that runs before the app's own remembers which boxes were typed in while nothing was listening,
and once the page is live it is told about each, exactly as if the letters had just been typed.
It remembers typing rather than comparing values, because a browser restores a form's contents
on reload and several boxes here save as they change — a restored date must never be saved over
a newer one. Tick-boxes and dropdowns are left alone for the same reason.

Pinned by `e2e/typed-before-load.spec.ts`: the script held back, real key presses, a round code
and the sign-up form, with a control that an untouched box is left as drawn.

## Sweep 17 — the second pass on Safari's engine, and what it was really saying (2026-10-11)

Eight tests still failed on WebKit after sweep 15. One was the app; seven were the bench or the
test, and each of those read as the app being broken until it was measured.

**The app.** The announcement card worked out "5 minutes ago" from its own clock while drawing.
On the player's Today it is drawn twice — by the server, then by the phone taking the page over,
because the folding list is a client component — so a notice on a boundary said two different
things. That is a hydration mismatch on the screen where #418 had last struck in CI.
`AnnouncementList` now words it once, on the server, and hands the words down. The clock guard
of sweep 11 could not see it: the card has no `"use client"` of its own and is only IMPORTED by a
file that does. The guard now follows imports from every marked file, to any depth.

**The bench, not the app** — written down so nobody chases them again:

- *The player's Today froze on its second visit.* Playwright's WebKit build on Windows offers
  `PushManager` and then never answers `getSubscription()`, which takes the page with it. Real
  Safari implements that call. `e2e/coverage.ts` removes `PushManager` on WebKit, so the switch
  reads "unavailable" there. **Push notifications are therefore not covered on WebKit**; the
  real-phone push test is still owed.
- *Every organizer-from-nothing spec waited out its whole timeout.* A run killed inside one of
  them leaves the organizer's club behind; the standing fixture's next start deletes the
  organizer (their address carries the mark) and the club — named after the person — is then
  found by nothing, while its tournament still makes the next "Morgan Starter" its organizer.
  `teardownOrganizer` now also finds the club through a tournament that address organizes.
- *A score entered on a casual round was missing after a reload.* The spec reloaded 100 ms after
  an in-app move, which cancelled the save still on its way. A person does not reload inside a
  tenth of a second; the spec now waits for the save it started.
- *A player taken out of a side bet went straight back in.* The row is saved a moment before the
  chip is redrawn; the spec tapped "the first ticked chip" again before it had changed. It now
  waits for the screen, as a person would.
